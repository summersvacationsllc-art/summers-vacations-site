### What's Biting
Trout fishing on Lake Taneycomo has improved noticeably over the last month. Good numbers of rainbows are showing throughout the upper lake compared to summer, and brown trout are making a strong fall spawning appearance, especially in the first mile of wadeable water below the dam. Anglers are finding success on scuds, small streamers, midges, and marabou jigs (black, sculpin, olive, or with an Indy orange head). Night fishing remains popular with medium streamers and small jerkbaits producing well after dark. On Table Rock, bass continue transitioning to deeper structure and points in the cooling, clear water.

## In Season
**Early Fall Transition.** Cooler overnight air and gradually cooling water have trout feeding more consistently through the day rather than only at the extremes. Stocked rainbows remain aggressive and less wary while browns are showing strongly for their fall run. On Table Rock, bass are sliding off summer shallows toward long points, creek channels, and deeper structure (20–40 ft) as they fatten ahead of winter. The gin-clear water demands clean, natural presentations and light line.

For the trout, start with natural colors and rotate from there: marabou jigs in black/sculpin/olive (especially with an orange head), zebra midges, scuds, hoppers, and egg patterns, plus mega and San Juan worms. Fly anglers are doing well with indicator rigs and midges or scuds in lighter flows, and hoppers still work on the warm afternoons. When more than one unit is running, drop to 2- to 4-lb test. When the water goes quiet, go lighter and slower. After dark, or when the current is ripping, reach for a black jig, a heavier rig or a jerkbait. And if you wade near the dam, stay well outside the 760-foot closed zone and watch for the water to rise when the units come on.

## Table Rock Lake
With the lake about 3.56 feet down, it's showing more bank than usual. Stumps and other hazards sit closer to the surface in the shallow coves, creek arms and near some ramps. Take it slow up the James, Kings and Long Creek arms, and expect a longer walk at a few launches.

The surface has cooled into the low- to mid-70s with excellent clarity. Bass have been hitting topwaters and soft plastics early. As the sun climbs, they move to jigs, live bait and drop-shot rigs around long points and gravel flats that drop into 20 to 40 feet, and onto offshore structure. Some mornings have produced solid numbers. The mixed bag is there too — walleye are holding on points in 20 to 25 feet, and crappie and spotted bass keep coming out of the deeper brush and around the docks.

## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
The trout water runs cold all year because it's fed by releases from the bottom of Table Rock, and steady stocking keeps it full of rainbows and improving numbers of browns. On this water, generation decides how you fish. When the dam is quiet overnight and early, you can wade, drift light line or fish an indicator. When the current builds, usually from afternoon into evening, you want weight on the line and a drift that works the seams and the structure.

From the dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water. It's artificial lures and flies only, and every rainbow between 12 and 20 inches goes back. Wade it during the low windows, outside the closed zone, with micro jigs and small flies. Save the pink worms under a float, the mega and San Juan worms and the egg patterns for the water below Fall Creek. Anyone keeping trout needs a trout permit. On moving water, drift boats and dock anglers do best by staying in the current breaks with heavier setups. Recent reports like Lilleys' (Oct 1) and John Sappington (early Oct) highlight marabou jigs in black, sculpin and olive (Indy orange head favorite), zebra midges in red and black, scuds, and night fishing with streamers or small jerkbaits. In water this clear, clean and natural wins, and a black jig after dark is still hard to beat. Call SWPA at 866-494-1993 before you head out.
