## In Season
**Early Fall Transition.** Cooler overnight air and gradually cooling water have trout feeding throughout the day rather than only at the extremes. Newly stocked rainbows remain active and less wary while browns are showing more frequently. On Table Rock the bass bite is shifting from pure summer shallows to points, creek arms, and deeper structure as fish fatten up ahead of winter. The gin-clear water rewards clean, natural presentations.

Target rainbows with micro jigs, zebra midges, scuds, hoppers, and worms — start natural then rotate colors. Fly anglers do well with indicator rigs in lighter flows; hoppers can still produce on warmer afternoons. When multiple units run, drop to 2–4 lb test and use weight to stay in the seams. During minimal flow, go lighter and slower. Black jigs after dark remain a strong option. On Taneycomo, wade only during confirmed low windows, stay well outside the closed zone below the dam, and watch for sudden rises when generation starts.

## Table Rock Lake
The lake is sitting approximately 3 ft below full pool, exposing more shoreline, stumps, and hazards in shallow coves, upper arms, and near some ramps. Boaters should use extra caution, especially in the James, Kings, and Long Creek arms where navigation can be tighter.

Surface water has been in the low- to mid-70s with excellent clarity in recent readings. Bass have responded well to topwaters and soft plastics early, then jigs, live bait, or drop-shot rigs around long points, gravel flats dropping into 20–40 ft, and offshore structure. Mixed-bag action continues with walleye on points in 20–25 ft and crappie or spotted bass coming from deeper brush and docks. Recent trips have produced solid numbers, including 20+ bass some mornings. The low water also means longer walks at certain launches.

## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
This cold tailwater stays productive year-round thanks to deep releases from Table Rock and steady stocking. Generation remains the primary tactic book: low or minimal flows overnight and early create prime windows for wading, light-line drifting, or indicator fishing, while stronger current (typically building afternoon into evening) calls for weighted rigs and precise drifts through seams and behind structure.

From Table Rock Dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water — **artificial lures and flies only; no soft plastics or scented/natural baits**. Rainbows 12–20 inches must be released. Save pink worms, PowerBait, or other soft plastics/scented baits for the water **below Fall Creek** and state that clearly. A trout permit is required to keep fish. Recent September patterns show strong action on micro jigs (brown, black, white, sculpin), mega worms, midges, scuds, and hoppers during low windows, with black jigs shining after dark. Water clarity is excellent — clean presentations win. Call 866-494-1993 for the latest SWPA schedule before you wade or launch.
