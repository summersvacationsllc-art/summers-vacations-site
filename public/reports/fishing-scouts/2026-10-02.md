## In Season
**Early Fall Transition.** Cooler overnight air and gradually cooling water have trout feeding more consistently through the day rather than only at the extremes. Newly stocked rainbows remain aggressive and less wary while browns are showing strongly for their fall run. On Table Rock, bass are sliding off summer shallows toward long points, creek channels, and deeper structure (20–40 ft) as they fatten ahead of winter. The gin-clear water demands clean, natural presentations and light line.

Target rainbows with marabou jigs in black/sculpin/olive, zebra midges, scuds, and small streamers — natural colors first, then rotate. Fly anglers do well with indicator rigs in lighter flows; night fishing with medium streamers or small jerkbaits shines after dark. When multiple units run, drop to 2–4 lb test and use enough weight to stay in the seams. During minimal flow, go lighter and slower for wading or dock drifting. Black or olive jigs with an orange head have been favorites per recent reports. On Taneycomo, wade only during confirmed low windows, stay well outside the closed zone below the dam, and watch for sudden rises when generation starts.

## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
This cold tailwater stays productive year-round thanks to deep releases from Table Rock and steady stocking. Generation remains the primary tactic book: low or minimal flows early create prime windows for wading, light-line drifting, or indicator fishing with micro jigs, midges, and scuds, while stronger current (typically building later in the day) calls for weighted rigs and precise drifts through seams and behind structure.

From Table Rock Dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water — **artificial lures and flies only; no soft plastics or scented/natural baits**. Rainbows 12–20 inches must be released. Save pink worms, PowerBait, mega worms, or other soft plastics/scented baits for the water **below Fall Creek** and state that clearly. A trout permit is required to keep fish below the US 65 bridge in many areas. Recent reports (Lilleys' Oct 1, John Sappington early Oct) highlight good rainbow numbers, strong brown activity (especially upper lake and first mile below dam), and success on marabou jigs (black, sculpin, olive), scuds, small streamers, midges, and night fishing with jerkbaits. Water clarity is excellent — clean presentations win. Night fishing has been particularly good for trophies. Call 866-494-1993 for the latest SWPA schedule before you wade or launch.
