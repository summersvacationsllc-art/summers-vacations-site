### What's Biting
Trout fishing on Lake Taneycomo has improved noticeably over the last month. Good numbers of rainbows are showing throughout the upper lake compared to summer, and brown trout are making a strong fall spawning appearance, especially in the first mile of wadeable water below the dam. Anglers are finding them on scuds, small streamers, midges, and marabou jigs (black, sculpin, olive, or with an Indy orange head). Night fishing remains popular with medium streamers and small jerkbaits producing well after dark. On Table Rock, bass continue transitioning to deeper structure in the clear, cooling water.

## In Season
**Early Fall Transition.** Cooler overnight air and gradually cooling water have trout feeding more consistently through the day rather than only at the extremes. Newly stocked rainbows remain aggressive and less wary while browns are showing strongly for their fall run. On Table Rock, bass are sliding off summer shallows toward long points, creek channels, and deeper structure (20–40 ft) as they fatten ahead of winter. The gin-clear water demands clean, natural presentations and light line.

Target rainbows with marabou jigs in black/sculpin/olive (especially with an orange head), zebra midges, scuds, and small streamers — natural colors first, then rotate. Fly anglers do well with indicator rigs in lighter flows; night fishing with medium streamers or small jerkbaits shines after dark. When multiple units run, drop to 2–4 lb test and use enough weight to stay in the seams. During minimal flow, go lighter and slower for wading or dock drifting. Black or olive jigs with an orange head have been favorites per recent reports. On Taneycomo, wade only during confirmed low windows, stay well outside the closed zone below the dam, and watch for sudden rises when generation starts.

## Table Rock Lake
The lake sits about 3.56 ft below full pool, exposing more shoreline, stumps, and hazards in shallow coves, upper arms, and near some ramps. Boaters should use extra caution, especially in the James, Kings, and Long Creek arms where navigation can tighten and docks may have limited access.

Surface water has cooled into the low- to mid-70s with excellent clarity in recent readings. Bass respond well to topwaters and soft plastics early in the day, then transition to jigs, live bait, or drop-shot rigs around long points, gravel flats dropping into 20–40 ft, and offshore structure. Mixed-bag action continues with walleye holding on points in 20–25 ft and crappie or spotted bass coming from deeper brush and docks. Recent patterns show solid catches possible, though the low water means longer walks at certain launches and slower navigation in the upper arms.

## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
This cold tailwater stays productive year-round thanks to deep releases from Table Rock and steady stocking. Generation remains the primary tactic book: low or minimal flows early create prime windows for wading, light-line drifting, or indicator fishing with micro jigs, midges, and scuds, while stronger current (typically building later in the day) calls for weighted rigs and precise drifts through seams and behind structure.

From Table Rock Dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water — **artificial lures and flies only; no soft plastics or scented/natural baits**. Rainbows 12–20 inches must be released. Save pink worms, PowerBait, mega worms, or other soft plastics/scented baits for the water **below Fall Creek**. A trout permit is required in many areas. Recent reports (Lilleys' Oct 1, John Sappington early Oct) highlight good rainbow numbers, strong brown activity (especially upper lake and first mile below dam), and success on marabou jigs (black, sculpin, olive), scuds, small streamers, midges, and night fishing with jerkbaits. Water clarity is excellent — clean presentations win. Night fishing has been particularly good for trophies. Call 866-494-1993 for the latest SWPA schedule before you wade or launch.
