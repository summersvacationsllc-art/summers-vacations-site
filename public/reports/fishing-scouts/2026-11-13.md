## In Season
**Early Fall Transition / Approaching Winter Drawdown.** Cooler nights and falling water levels have trout feeding more consistently while Table Rock bass have moved off shallow summer structure to deeper points, creek channels, gravel flats, and offshore lies as they pack on weight for winter. Stocked rainbows stay eager; brown trout are becoming more active in the upper Taneycomo stretches. The gin-clear water demands precise, natural presentations and light line during lower flows.

On Table Rock, target bass in 20–40 ft with jigs, drop-shot rigs, or live bait around long points and structure. Walleye hold on points in 20–25 ft; crappie and spotted bass work deeper brush and docks. On Taneycomo, natural colors and finesse shine in low water: marabou jigs (black/sculpin/olive), zebra midges, scuds, and small streamers. Fly anglers score with indicator rigs in lighter current. Add weight and move to 2–4 lb test when generation increases. Black jigs remain productive after dark. ## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
This cold tailwater remains one of the most consistent trout fisheries in the Midwest thanks to steady MDC stocking and cold releases from the bottom of Table Rock Dam. Generation is the primary tactic book: low or no generation allows lighter rigs, wading, or indicator fishing in the seams and soft water; stronger flows demand added weight, drifting the current breaks, and 2–4 lb test.

From Table Rock Dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water — **artificial lures and flies only; no soft plastics or scented/natural baits allowed**. Rainbows 12–20 inches must be released. Save pink worms, PowerBait, or other soft plastics/scented baits for water **below Fall Creek**. A trout permit is required in many areas. Recent reports show solid numbers of rainbows with increasing brown trout activity in the upper stretches on marabou jigs, midges, scuds, small streamers, and eggs. Water clarity is generally excellent. Always call 866-494-1993 for the latest SWPA generation schedule before heading out and stay clear of the closed zone below the dam.
