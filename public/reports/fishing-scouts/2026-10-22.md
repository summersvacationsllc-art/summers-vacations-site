## In Season
**Early Fall Transition.** Cooler nights and gradually cooling water have trout feeding more consistently through the day rather than just dawn and dusk. Stocked rainbows remain aggressive while browns are showing more strongly as their fall run builds. On Table Rock, bass have moved off summer shallows toward long points, creek channels, and deeper structure (20–40 ft) as they build reserves for winter. The gin-clear water rewards clean, natural presentations and light line.

For the trout, natural colors and finesse rule: Berkley Pink PowerWorm (2 lb test preferred when off), marabou jigs in black/sculpin/olive, zebra midges (#14-20 red/black), scuds, small streamers, and Gulp eggs (orange/pink). Fly anglers do well with indicator rigs in lighter flows. When current increases, add weight and drop to 2- to 4-lb test. Black jigs after dark remain effective. On Taneycomo, wade only during confirmed low windows, stay well outside the closed zone below the dam, and watch for the water to rise when units come on.

## Table Rock Lake
With the lake just over 4 feet below full pool, more shoreline, stumps, and hazards are exposed in the shallow coves, creek arms, and near some ramps. Take it slow up the James, Kings, and Long Creek arms, and check your launch point.

Surface water has cooled into the upper 60s with gin-clear visibility. Bass respond well to topwaters and soft plastics early, then shift to jigs, live bait, or drop-shot rigs around long points, gravel flats dropping into 20–40 feet, and offshore structure. Mixed-bag action includes walleye holding on points in 20–25 feet and crappie or spotted bass coming from deeper brush and around docks. Recent patterns show solid catches when presentations stay clean in this clear, cooling water.

## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
This cold tailwater stays productive year-round thanks to deep releases from Table Rock and steady stocking. Generation remains the primary tactic book: minimal or low flows early create excellent windows for wading, light-line drifting, or indicator fishing with micro jigs, midges, scuds, worms, or Gulp eggs, while stronger current (often building in the afternoon and evening) calls for weighted rigs and precise drifts through seams and behind structure.

From Table Rock Dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water — **artificial lures and flies only; no soft plastics or scented/natural baits**. Rainbows 12–20 inches must be released. Save pink worms, PowerBait, Gulp eggs or other soft plastics/scented baits for the water **below Fall Creek**. A trout permit is required in many areas. Recent reports (Oct 20 Lilleys journal and Oct tackle.net) highlight slow dock fishing but better boat catches on worms and eggs, very good wading with Pink PowerWorm when off, and a notable 28.25-inch rainbow. Water clarity is good to murky in places — clean, natural presentations win. Call 866-494-1993 for the latest SWPA schedule before you wade.
