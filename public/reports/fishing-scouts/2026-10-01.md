## In Season
**Early Fall Transition.** Cooler mornings and gradually cooling water have trout feeding throughout the day. Newly stocked rainbows remain active and less wary while browns show more frequently. On Table Rock, bass are shifting from pure summer shallows toward points, creek arms, and deeper structure as they fatten up for winter. The gin-clear water rewards clean, natural presentations.

Target rainbows with micro jigs, zebra midges, scuds, hoppers, and worms — start with natural colors then rotate. Fly anglers do well with indicator rigs in lighter flows; hoppers can still produce on warmer afternoons. When multiple units run, drop to 2–4 lb test and use weight to stay in the seams. During minimal flow, go lighter and slower. Black jigs after dark remain a strong option. On Taneycomo, wade only during confirmed low windows, stay well outside the closed zone below the dam, and watch for sudden rises when generation starts.

## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
This cold tailwater stays productive year-round thanks to deep releases from Table Rock and steady stocking. Generation remains the primary tactic book: low or minimal flows overnight and early create prime windows for wading, light-line drifting, or indicator fishing, while stronger current (typically building afternoon into evening) calls for weighted rigs and precise drifts through seams and behind structure.

From Table Rock Dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water — **artificial lures and flies only; no soft plastics or scented/natural baits**. Rainbows 12–20 inches must be released. Save pink worms, PowerBait, or other soft plastics/scented baits for the water **below Fall Creek**. A trout permit is required to keep fish. Recent patterns show strong action on micro jigs (brown, black, white, sculpin), midges, scuds, and hoppers during low windows, with black jigs shining after dark. Water clarity is excellent — clean presentations win. Call 866-494-1993 for the latest SWPA schedule before you wade or launch.
