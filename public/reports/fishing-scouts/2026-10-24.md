## In Season
**Early Fall Transition.** Cooler mornings and gradually cooling water have trout feeding more steadily through the day. Stocked rainbows remain aggressive while browns are building for their fall run and becoming more territorial. On Table Rock, bass have moved off shallow summer haunts toward long points, creek channels, and deeper structure (20–40 ft) as they fatten up for winter. The gin-clear conditions demand clean, natural presentations and light line.

For Taneycomo trout, natural colors and finesse win: Berkley Pink PowerWorm (2-lb test best when flows are off), marabou jigs in black/sculpin/olive, zebra midges, scuds, small streamers, and Gulp eggs (orange/pink). Fly anglers do well with indicator rigs during lighter flows. Add weight and drop to 2–4 lb test when current increases. Black jigs after dark remain effective. Wade only during confirmed low windows, stay well outside the closed zone below the dam, and watch for rising water when units come online.

## Table Rock Lake
At just over 4 ft below full pool, more shoreline, stumps, and hazards are exposed in the coves, creek arms, and near some ramps. Boat slowly, especially up the James, Kings, and Long Creek arms, and double-check launch conditions.

Surface water has cooled into the upper 60s with exceptional clarity. Mixed-bag action includes walleye on points in 20–25 ft and crappie/spotted bass from deeper brush and docks. Clean presentations in this clear, cooling water continue to produce solid catches.

## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
This cold tailwater fed by deep releases from Table Rock stays productive year-round thanks to consistent stocking. Generation is the primary tactic book: low or minimal flows early create excellent windows for wading, light-line drifting, or indicator fishing with micro jigs, midges, scuds, worms, or Gulp eggs. When current builds (typically afternoon into evening), switch to weighted rigs and target seams and structure.

From Table Rock Dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water — **artificial lures and flies only; no soft plastics or scented/natural baits**. Rainbows 12–20 inches must be released. Save pink worms, PowerBait, Gulp eggs, or other soft plastics/scented baits for water **below Fall Creek**. A trout permit is required in many sections. Recent reports highlight improving rainbow numbers, active browns in the upper lake, slow dock fishing but better boat catches on worms and eggs, and productive wading with Pink PowerWorm when flows are off. Water clarity is generally good. Call 866-494-1993 for the latest SWPA schedule before heading out.
