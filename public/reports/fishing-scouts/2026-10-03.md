## In Season
**Early Fall Transition.** Cooler nights and gradually cooling water have trout feeding more consistently throughout the day. Newly stocked rainbows remain aggressive while brown trout are becoming more active in their fall patterns. On Table Rock, bass are moving off summer shallows toward long points, creek channels, and deeper structure (20–40 ft) as they build reserves for winter. The gin-clear water rewards clean, natural presentations and light line.

Target rainbows with marabou jigs (black, sculpin, olive), zebra midges, scuds, and small streamers — start natural then rotate colors. Fly anglers do well with indicator rigs during lighter flows; night fishing with medium streamers or small jerkbaits has been productive after dark. When multiple units run, drop to 2–4 lb test and add weight to stay in the seams. During minimal flow, go lighter and slower for wading or dock drifting. Black or olive jigs with an orange head remain favorites. On Taneycomo, wade only during confirmed low windows, stay well outside the closed zone below the dam, and watch for sudden rises when generation starts.

## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
This cold tailwater stays productive year-round thanks to deep releases from Table Rock and steady stocking. Generation remains the primary tactic book: low or minimal flows early create prime windows for wading, light-line drifting, or indicator fishing with micro jigs, midges, and scuds, while stronger current (typically building later) calls for weighted rigs and precise drifts through seams and behind structure.

From Table Rock Dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water — **artificial lures and flies only; no soft plastics or scented/natural baits**. Rainbows 12–20 inches must be released. Save pink worms, PowerBait, mega worms, or other soft plastics/scented baits for the water **below Fall Creek**. A trout permit is required in many areas. Recent reports (Lilleys' early October, John Sappington) highlight good rainbow numbers, strong brown activity (especially upper lake and first mile below dam), and success on marabou jigs (black, sculpin, olive), scuds, small streamers, midges, and night fishing with jerkbaits. Water clarity is excellent — clean presentations win. Night fishing has been particularly good for trophies. Call 866-494-1993 for the latest SWPA schedule before you wade or launch.
