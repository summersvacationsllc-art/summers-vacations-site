## In Season
**Early Fall Transition.** Cooler nights and gradually cooling water have trout feeding more consistently through the day. Newly stocked rainbows remain aggressive while browns are showing strongly for their fall run. On Table Rock, bass are moving off summer shallows toward long points, creek channels, and deeper structure (20–40 ft) as they build reserves for winter. The gin-clear water rewards clean, natural presentations and light line.

For the trout, start with natural colors and rotate: marabou jigs in black, sculpin or olive (orange head favorite), zebra midges, scuds, and small streamers. Fly anglers do well with indicator rigs during lighter flows; night fishing with medium streamers or small jerkbaits has been productive. When multiple units run, drop to 2–4 lb test and add weight. During minimal flow, go lighter and slower for wading or dock drifting. Black jigs after dark remain effective. On Taneycomo, wade only during confirmed low windows, stay well outside the closed zone below the dam, and watch for sudden rises when generation starts.

## Table Rock Lake
The lake is nearly 4 feet below full pool, exposing more shoreline, stumps and hazards in shallow coves, upper arms and near some ramps. Boaters should use extra caution, particularly in the James, Kings and Long Creek arms where navigation can tighten and certain launches require a longer walk.

Surface water has cooled into the mid- to upper 60s with excellent clarity. Bass respond well to topwaters and soft plastics early, then shift to jigs, live bait or drop-shot rigs around long points, gravel flats dropping into 20–40 ft, and offshore structure. Mixed-bag action includes walleye on points in 20–25 ft and crappie or spotted bass from deeper brush and docks. Recent patterns show solid catches are possible when presentations match the clear, cooling conditions.

## Lake Taneycomo — The Trout Water
This cold tailwater stays productive year-round thanks to deep releases from Table Rock and steady stocking. Generation remains the primary tactic book: lower or minimal flows early create prime windows for wading, light-line drifting or indicator fishing with micro jigs, midges and scuds, while stronger current (often building later) calls for weighted rigs and precise drifts through seams and behind structure.

From Table Rock Dam down to Fall Creek is trophy water — **artificial lures and flies only; no soft plastics or scented/natural baits**. Rainbows 12–20 inches must be released. Save pink worms, PowerBait, mega worms or other soft plastics/scented baits for the water **below Fall Creek**. A trout permit is required in many areas. Recent reports highlight good rainbow numbers, strong brown activity (especially upper lake and first mile below dam), and success on marabou jigs (black/sculpin/olive, Indy orange head favorite), zebra midges in red/black, scuds, and night fishing with streamers or small jerkbaits. Water clarity is excellent — clean presentations win. Call 866-494-1993 for the latest SWPA schedule before you wade or launch.
